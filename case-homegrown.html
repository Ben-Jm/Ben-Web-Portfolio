Fintech Payments mPOS & QR Mobile-first Greenfield product

Designing a homegrown payments app for traders and enterprises.

A Botswana-built digital payment platform launching to market in 2026. Built for small traders through to medium enterprises, with sending, receiving, and payment via mobile point-of-sale (mPOS) and QR code.

Context

Building payments for two audiences at once.

The solution was conceived as a homegrown alternative for Botswana's payments market — a single platform serving everyone from informal small-scale traders working out of stalls and pop-ups, through to medium enterprises with formal back-office operations. The founders' brief was to make sending, receiving, and accepting payments as simple as a phone call, with a handy mobile point-of-sale device (mPOS) and QR code that "worked anywhere."

The design challenge sat in the gap between those two audiences. A polished enterprise-grade UI would alienate first-time digital traders. A radically simplified consumer flow wouldn't give enterprises the controls they need. The product had to feel native to both — without forking into two separate apps.

Research

Working from founder vision and the realities of Botswana's trading culture.

I worked closely with the founders — who knew their market intimately — and grounded the design in observable patterns of how small traders and enterprises in Botswana already handle payments today: cash-first, mobile-money-supplemented, with WhatsApp serving as the de-facto support channel and word-of-mouth as the primary trust mechanism.

01 · Method

Founder workshops

Structured sessions with the founders to translate the product vision into specific user roles, transaction types, edge cases, and success criteria. Surfaced the trade-offs between simplicity and capability that the design would need to navigate.

Founder vision
02 · Method

Market & competitive scan

Reviewed how existing local players (mobile money, bank apps, USSD services) and regional fintechs (M-Pesa, MTN MoMo, regional QR-pay platforms) handle the same transactional moments — what's familiar to Botswana users and what's a learnable new pattern.

Local & regional payments products
03 · Method

Behavioural grounding

Mapped the everyday payment behaviours of both audiences — how a market trader prices goods on the fly, how an enterprise reconciles end-of-day takings, where each gets stuck or improvises today. Used to anchor design decisions in real-world friction, not theoretical UX.

Insights

What the research surfaced.

The discovery work pointed at a small set of consistent realities for the Botswana market — patterns that became the constraints the design had to honour.

01

Trust beats features.

For first-time digital-payment users, the design earns trust through explicit confirmation, plain-language receipts, and visible amounts — not through feature density. A "fast and secure" promise has to be felt at every screen, not just claimed in the marketing.

02

Two audiences, one mental model.

Small traders and enterprises don't need different apps — they need the same core flows with different controls layered on top. An enterprise admin view, role-based permissions, and reconciliation tools sit on the same flow a market trader uses to take a single payment.

03

Connectivity is variable; the design has to know that.

Outside Gaborone and Francistown, network coverage drops. A payment flow that works only on strong 4G fails the very users the solution is trying to reach. Offline-tolerance and clear failure states became as important as the happy path.
